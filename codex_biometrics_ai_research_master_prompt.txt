
MASTER PROMPT FOR CODEX
=======================

ROLE
----
You are an autonomous research engineer working on an academic biometrics + AI research project at the University of Southern Denmark (SDU).

This is NOT a generic software-development task. Your job is to behave like a careful reproducibility researcher: read the literature, verify sources, inspect original repositories, acquire only legally/ethically usable datasets and models, create a clean research repository, reproduce one or two relevant published experiments as faithfully as possible, and then prepare the codebase for our own proposed experiment.

Do not merely give me a plan. EXECUTE the plan in the repository as far as the environment and dataset/model licenses allow.

The project is based on the research presentation "When Your Face Becomes the Password", whose central question is:

    Can a low-knowledge, identity-centric neural attacker extract identity information
    from multiple protected DNN face templates without knowing their secret keys?

The proposed hypothesis is:

    "Identity leakage from individually protected templates can be weak, yet collectively
    exploitable: a key-agnostic neural set model can aggregate multiple independently
    protected observations and recover increasingly usable identity information without
    access to the secret keys."

The intended pipeline is:

    face image
      -> face-recognition feature extractor (primarily ArcFace)
      -> cancelable biometric protection (BioHash / MLP-Hash / IoM, etc.)
      -> protected template
      -> key-agnostic learned attacker
      -> identity representation
      -> identity matching / linkage evaluation

The main experimental axes proposed in the presentation are:

    1 exposure
    2 exposures
    5 exposures
    10 exposures

plus:

    - unseen-key generalization
    - identity recovery / identity matching
    - aggregation-method comparisons
    - optional cross-scheme transfer

The first research milestone, however, is to understand and reproduce the relevant literature before attempting the novel experiment.

       - license/access restrictions

   Never circumvent dataset registrations, licenses, authentication, robots restrictions,

       - create the scripts/configuration needed to use it once downloaded,

       - use a clearly labelled fallback/smoke-test dataset only for engineering validation.

4. USE ONLY PUBLIC/AUTHORIZED RESEARCH DATA
   Do not scrape random private individuals.
   Do not create a real-world identity lookup system.
   Do not test against private databases.
   Restrict experiments to public/authorized academic datasets and synthetic data.

5. DO NOT COMMIT DATASETS OR LARGE MODEL WEIGHTS TO GIT
   Put them under ignored directories and provide download/setup scripts.

   Every meaningful experiment should be runnable with a command and a config file.
   Do not leave essential steps only in notebooks.

9. CONTROL RANDOMNESS
   Seed Python, NumPy, and PyTorch where possible.
   Record all seeds.
   Run multiple seeds for learned attack models where computationally feasible.

10. NEVER FABRICATE A RESULT

A. CENTRAL BASELINE PAPER
-------------------------

"Benchmarking of Cancelable Biometrics for Deep Templates"
Hatef Otroshi Shahreza et al.
Journal on Image and Video Processing, version of record published 2026.
Earlier preprint: arXiv:2302.13286

Known official/open-source code:
    https://github.com/otroshi/benchmark_cb

Paper:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13286
    https://doi.org/10.1186/s13640-025-00679-y

This paper is extremely important because it benchmarks several cancelable biometric
protection schemes on DNN templates, including:

    - BioHashing
    - MLP-Hash
    - Bloom Filters
    - IoM-URP
    - IoM-GRP
    - Rand-Hash baseline

For face recognition it uses:
    - ArcFace
    - MOBIO face data

The presentation cites approximately the following face recognition EER values as targets
to cross-check against the original paper/code, NOT as values to hard-code:

    BioHash     ~0.02%
    MLP-Hash    ~0.02%
    Bloom       ~2.19%
    IoM-GRP     ~0.02%
    IoM-URP     ~0.72%
    Rand-Hash   ~0.02%

The presentation also highlights a BioHash face mutual-information example:

    unknown-key MI ~39.63
    known-key MI   ~98.81

Again, VERIFY these values in the original source before putting them in reproduction tables.

D. IDENTITY-CENTRIC LEAKAGE / FACELINKGEN
-----------------------------------------

"FaceLinkGen: Rethinking Identity Leakage in Privacy-Preserving Face Recognition with
Identity Extraction"
Wenqi Guo and Shan Du
arXiv:2602.02914, 2026

Paper:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02914

This is conceptually central to our proposed work.

Core idea:
    Instead of requiring pixel-level reconstruction of the original face, learn to recover an
    identity-discriminative embedding from a protected representation.

The paper uses ArcFace-domain identity supervision and evaluates linkage/matching and
regeneration. It studies privacy-preserving face-representation systems such as:
    - PartialFace
    - MinusFace
    - FracFace

Important paper details to verify:
    - teacher/student setup
    - exact loss
    - student architecture
    - datasets
    - train/validation split
    - query/key construction
    - linkage metrics
    - verification metrics
    - low-knowledge setting
    - whether official code exists

Do NOT assume an official FaceLinkGen repository exists. Search carefully. If there is no
official released implementation, state that and implement a reproduction directly from the
paper.

The paper reports using:
    - CASIA-WebFace subset for distillation
    - LFW for evaluation/distribution shift
    - TPDNE synthetic faces for some evaluation
    - Antelopev2 / ArcFace-compatible identity embeddings
    - Arc2Face for optional face regeneration

Official Arc2Face implementation:
    https://github.com/foivospar/Arc2Face

IMPORTANT:
For our research, the most important FaceLinkGen component is IDENTITY EXTRACTION AND
LINKAGE, not photorealistic face regeneration. Regeneration is optional and should not block
the project.

RESEARCH QUESTION
-----------------

Can identity information be extracted from multiple independently protected DNN face

CORE THREAT MODEL
-----------------

The attacker:

    - public face data / public training identities
    - public knowledge of the protection scheme category/algorithm if this matches our final

DOES NOT HAVE ACCESS TO:

    - test-subject secret keys
    - privileged internal key material
    - raw enrollment face corresponding to the attacked protected template

The presentation additionally frames the attacker as low-knowledge with respect to internal

Create at least two explicit levels:

        scheme family known,
        secret keys unknown,
        test keys unseen during training.

    K1 / STRONGER KNOWLEDGE CONTROL:
        scheme hyperparameters known,
        keys still unknown.

13. DATA GENERATION FOR MULTI-EXPOSURE
======================================================================

For every image x of identity y:

    e = ArcFace(x)

For every exposure j:

    k_j ~ independent key distribution
    t_j = Protect(e, k_j)

An "exposure" means an independently protected observation produced under a different key
unless the literature/protocol motivates another definition.

Create experiments with:

    n = 1
    n = 2
    n = 5
    n = 10

If enough images per identity exist, consider two distinct sources of multiplicity:

A. SAME underlying image / different protection keys
B. DIFFERENT images of same identity / different protection keys

These test different questions and MUST NOT be conflated.

Preferred main experiment:
    different face samples + independently sampled keys,
because it better approximates repeated enrollments/exposures across systems.

But also include a controlled same-image/different-key experiment if feasible, because it
isolates the effect of the protection transformation.

Write:
    docs/protocols/multi_exposure.md

with exact sampling rules.

14. TRAIN/VAL/TEST SPLITTING
======================================================================

IDENTITY DISJOINTNESS IS MANDATORY.

No person in test may occur in attack-model training.

Create deterministic split manifests:
    data/processed/splits/train.csv
    data/processed/splits/val.csv
    data/processed/splits/test.csv

The attack should learn a transformation that generalizes to unseen identities, not memorize
identity labels.

UNSEEN-KEY GENERALIZATION
-------------------------

Also split keys:

    train key pool
    validation key pool
    test key pool

The final unseen-key experiment must use keys that were never used during attack-model
training.

This is central to the claim "key-agnostic".

We need at least four diagnostic conditions:

    A. seen identities + seen keys      # sanity upper bound / debugging
    B. unseen identities + seen keys
    C. seen identities + unseen keys
    D. unseen identities + unseen keys # MAIN evaluation

Do not present condition A as evidence of generalization.

15. ATTACK MODEL BASELINES
======================================================================

Implement strong simple baselines before sophisticated set models.

SINGLE-TEMPLATE BASELINES
-------------------------

Input:
    one protected template

Models:
    1. linear projection
    2. 2-layer MLP
    3. deeper residual MLP if useful

Target:
    original unprotected ArcFace identity embedding

Loss candidates to evaluate:
    - cosine embedding loss
    - normalized MSE
    - combination

Choose based on validation and document.

MULTI-TEMPLATE NON-LEARNED BASELINES
------------------------------------

Given protected templates t_1...t_n, test:

    - raw mean pooling, then MLP
    - raw max pooling, then MLP
    - per-template encoder -> mean
    - per-template encoder -> max

MULTI-TEMPLATE LEARNED SET MODELS
---------------------------------

Implement in increasing complexity:

    1. DeepSets-style encoder:
           z_i = phi(t_i)
           z = mean(z_i)
           output = rho(z)

    2. Attention pooling

    3. Set Transformer

IMPORTANT:
Models must be permutation invariant with respect to the order of exposures unless there is
a scientifically justified ordering.

All models should support a mask so n can vary within a batch.

16. PRIMARY OUTPUT REPRESENTATION
======================================================================

The attacker's primary output should be an identity embedding aligned to the original ArcFace
space.

Why:
    - it directly tests identity leakage,
    - it avoids requiring photorealistic reconstruction,
    - it allows local matching metrics,
    - it connects directly to FaceLinkGen's identity-centric thesis.

For test sample/set S belonging to identity y:

    z_hat = attacker(S)

Compare z_hat against:
    - ArcFace embeddings from held-out real images of y
    - embeddings from other identities

Evaluate whether z_hat is identity-discriminative.

Do not train a closed-set classifier over test identities and call that identity recovery.

30. SCIENTIFIC END STATE
======================================================================

The repository should ultimately let us answer a precise question, not merely demonstrate
that a neural network can fit protected vectors.

The desired final evidence is a plot/table showing, for held-out identities and unseen
protection keys:

    Number of protected observations:
        1     2     5     10

versus identity-recovery quality such as:
        cosine similarity
        verification EER
        TAR@FAR
        top-1 linkage

for one or more cancelable protection schemes.

The strongest scientifically meaningful outcome would be evidence that:

    performance improves systematically as independently protected observations of the same
    identity are aggregated,

while:

    the attacker never receives the corresponding secret keys,
    test identities are unseen during training,
    test keys are unseen during training,
    and no metadata leakage explains the result.

A negative result is also scientifically valuable. If multi-exposure does NOT improve
identity recovery under a rigorously controlled protocol, report it faithfully and investigate
why.

The objective is not to force the hypothesis to be true. The objective is to build the
cleanest reproducible experiment capable of falsifying it.
